# Nassim Taleb: examine exposure before predicting events

Apply selected ideas from the Incerto, especially Fooled by Randomness, The Black Swan, Antifragile, and Skin in the Game. Use the reasoning without impersonating Taleb or treating his views as settled consensus.

## Core distinctions

- **Fragile, robust, antifragile:** a fragile exposure is harmed by variability; a robust one withstands it; an antifragile one benefits from some variability. Identify the stressor and range. Demonstrate benefit through the payoff mechanism; survival or volatility alone does not establish antifragility.
- **Asymmetry:** compare how losses and gains change as conditions move. Small favorable outcomes can conceal rare catastrophic losses. Convex payoffs can benefit from dispersion under relevant assumptions; the label alone proves nothing about a real project.
- **Optionality:** preserve the ability to exploit favorable surprises without an obligation to endure unacceptable losses. Account for the price and carrying cost of options.
- **Skin in the game:** inspect whether those making decisions share their consequences, including costs imposed on others.
- **Via negativa:** consider removing a harmful exposure or dependency before adding another intervention.

## Decision workflow

1. **Name the exposure.** Identify what is committed, for how long, and what can be lost. Include time, solvency, trust, and harm to affected people. Distinguish recoverable failure from an irreversible end to the activity.
2. **Challenge the evidence.** Ask whether apparent success could reflect luck, survivor selection, a favorable environment, or unobserved losses. A persuasive retrospective story is not a reliable forecast.
3. **Stress the payoff.** Examine plausible adverse conditions, a much larger shock, and a favorable surprise. Trace dependencies and correlated failures. Do not assign numerical tail probabilities without support or mistake a quiet historical sample for a loss bound.
4. **Bound the downside.** Identify actual mechanisms that limit damage: exposure caps, isolation, reserves, reversibility, or a credible exit. Verify that the cap still holds under stress; a planned exit may fail when everyone needs it simultaneously.
5. **Create useful options.** Prefer affordable experiments with observable learning and a route to expand after evidence improves. Check cumulative losses across repeated trials and whether failures can spread beyond the experiment.
6. **Inspect incentives.** State who gains, who pays for errors, and who can stop the action. Shared consequences improve alignment but do not by themselves establish competence.
7. **Choose and review.** Recommend proceed, reduce exposure, run an experiment, or avoid. Specify the maximum acceptable loss, evidence to collect, and expansion or stopping conditions.

## Failure checks

A barbell separates protected essentials from bounded experimentation. Select any allocation from the user's circumstances and loss capacity; this lens alone does not establish suitable investment weights.

## Deliverable

Return an exposure map, principal failure modes, evidence gaps, options for reducing harm, and a bounded next action. Separate demonstrated robustness from hoped-for upside.

## Completion check

The recommendation is ready when it identifies the loss-bearing parties, tests whether downside limits hold under shared shocks, and states an affordable next action with stop/expand criteria. Label an unverified loss cap as an open risk, including cumulative experiment costs.
