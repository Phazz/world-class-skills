# Portfolio management: allocate scarce capacity across commitments

Manage the portfolio as a whole, including ongoing operations, experiments, and proposed projects. Optimize for the user's stated objectives, constraints, and time horizon. A collection of individually attractive projects can still exceed capacity or share the same failure mode.

## Required context

Establish the portfolio's purpose, decision authority, review horizon, available money and capacity, essential obligations, and acceptable exposure. Use the current project register, recent evidence, and prior decisions. For each initiative seek:

- Identifier, owner, stage, intended beneficiary and outcome, and strategic rationale.
- Actual spending and capacity use, remaining cost and effort, and relevant resource bottlenecks.
- Evidence of value, uncertainty, next milestone or experiment, and success or stopping criteria.
- Dependencies, exit cost, major risks, and source links with observation dates.

Mark missing data. Do not infer capacity from headcount alone or convert uncertain benefits into precise financial returns. If critical information is missing, produce a conditional review and the specific data request rather than a confident allocation.

## Review process

1. **Reconcile the baseline.** Compare current evidence with the last review. Separate approved commitments, proposals, actual results, and forecasts. Record source freshness and explain material changes.
2. **Apply constraints first.** Reserve capacity for essential operations and obligations. Identify budget, staffing, dependency, and exposure limits. Keep resources in compatible units and include transition costs and time already promised elsewhere.
3. **Compare future choices.** Evaluate continuing each initiative against stopping, pausing, simplifying, or running a smaller experiment. Ignore irrecoverable spending as a justification to continue, while counting genuine future exit costs. Compare new proposals with the work they would displace.
4. **Inspect the combination.** Look for common customers, platforms, suppliers, assumptions, and people that create correlated exposure. Check sequencing, mutually exclusive commitments, and double-counted benefits from dependent projects. Preserve useful reserves rather than assuming every resource should be fully allocated.
5. **Construct feasible alternatives.** Present a recommended allocation and a credible alternative when the tradeoff matters. Show what expands and what gives way. Reconcile totals against available resources; do not average unlike measures into an unexplained score.
6. **Resolve conflicting advice.** State the disagreement and the governing constraint. For example, uncertain upside may justify a bounded experiment even when predictable returns are weak, provided the loss is affordable and essential commitments remain protected. Define in advance what evidence would justify expansion.
7. **Recommend decisions.** Assign start, continue, expand, simplify, pause, or stop with rationale, resource change, evidence needed, and a review trigger. No change is a valid decision; avoid constant reallocation based on noise.
8. **Maintain continuity.** Record proposed and approved decisions separately, including rationale and superseded assumptions. Recommend changes without executing cancellations, assigning people, spending, or contacting owners unless authorized.

For intake, a periodic review, or an unattended run, read [review modes](references/review-modes.md) and select the matching scope. Use the full process for a one-off allocation decision.

## Deliverable

Lead with decisions needed and material changes. Include a compact table of initiative, evidence date, recommendation, reason, resource change, dependency or risk, and next gate. Finish with reconciled capacity totals, key uncertainties, and the decision log entries proposed for approval.
