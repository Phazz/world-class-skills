# When setting objectives and key results

State the objective as the result to achieve and key results as verifiable evidence of progress or completion. Keep the set focused. For each key result, specify a measure, baseline when available, target, deadline, and source of evidence. Label missing baselines rather than inventing them.

Use these modern implementation checks: distinguish work plans from success measures; pair speed or volume with a relevant quality measure; and make dependencies visible. A delivery milestone can be useful, but explain what outcome it enables. Do not treat a later OKR convention, such as a universal stretch-goal score, as a rule from Grove.
