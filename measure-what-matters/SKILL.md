# Measure What Matters: turn priorities into evidence

Apply a selective synthesis of John Doerr's *Measure What Matters*. Use OKRs to connect important goals to evidence of achievement and recurring learning. Attribute the Intel origins to Andy Grove and the book's articulation and popularization to Doerr.

## Guiding principles

Use focus, alignment, commitment, tracking, and stretching to assess the practice. An objective states what matters; key results establish how achievement will be recognized. The framework helps execute choices but does not supply a missing strategy.

Distinguish **committed** goals, which the team intends and plans to deliver, from **aspirational** goals, which deliberately stretch beyond an established path. Agree on the distinction before work begins. Do not relabel a missed commitment as aspirational afterward or assume every goal should target partial completion.

Complement measurement with **CFRs: conversations, feedback, and recognition**. Discuss progress and obstacles, exchange timely and actionable feedback, and recognize specific contributions. A dashboard cannot replace these interactions.

## Build or critique the OKRs

1. **Choose the change.** Identify the user or business problem, planning horizon, and decision owner. Explain why this deserves scarce attention and what will receive less attention. Keep the set small enough to guide tradeoffs.
2. **Write the objective.** Describe a meaningful desired state in plain language. Challenge objectives whose completion would not matter to a customer, the organization, or its mission.
3. **Define proof.** Write key results that collectively support the objective. Specify the metric or verifiable condition, population, baseline when available, target, deadline, owner, and evidence source. Separate implementation tasks from proof that they worked. Label unknown baselines and proposed targets explicitly.
4. **Test sufficiency and incentives.** Could every key result be achieved while the objective still fails? Could someone improve the number by harming quality or excluding difficult cases? Revise the measures or add a relevant guardrail.
5. **Align across teams.** Explain how the objective contributes to a larger priority without mechanically copying a manager's goals. Surface dependencies and conflicting incentives. A dependency is not a commitment until the responsible team agrees.
6. **Check capacity and ambition.** Identify resources, competing obligations, and critical assumptions. Preserve essential operations. Make stretch uncertainty explicit without allowing it to silently displace promised work.
7. **Operate the cycle.** Set a review cadence appropriate to the work. At each check-in, distinguish actual progress, confidence of completion, blockers, and decisions needed. Change the work when evidence warrants it; record any goal revision and its reason.
8. **Close with learning.** Compare results with the original definition, explain misses and unexpected success, and decide what to continue, stop, or change. Avoid treating a score as a complete performance review or an automatic compensation formula.

## Working format

Provide an objective, its rationale and committed/aspirational designation, followed by a compact table:

| Key result | Baseline → target or completion condition | Deadline | Owner | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then list dependencies, relevant guardrails, and the next review. Keep initiatives in a separate short list when needed. Do not imply that an OKR has been adopted merely because it has been drafted.

## Example check

“Launch onboarding” describes work. “New users can reach their first useful result reliably” describes an intended change. Its key results might measure successful completion, time to value, and error rate. Set numerical targets only after inspecting actual baselines and constraints.

## Completion check

The draft is ready when each key result has a completion condition, deadline, evidence source, and owner or explicit gap; the set passes the sufficiency and incentive checks; and commitments are distinguished from aspirations. Keep unknown baselines and unaccepted dependencies visible.
