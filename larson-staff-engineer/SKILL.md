# Staff Engineer: multiply technical impact

Apply a selective synthesis of Will Larson's *Staff Engineer: Leadership beyond the management track* (2021). Begin with the organization's need and the person's actual authority rather than assuming that seniority means writing more complex code.

## Establish the role

Use Larson's archetypes as diagnostic lenses:

- **Tech Lead:** guide a team's technical direction and execution with its manager.
- **Architect:** sustain direction and quality within an important technical domain.
- **Solver:** resolve especially difficult, important problems with a credible handoff.
- **Right Hand:** extend an executive's reach through delegated context and authority.

Determine which role the organization actually needs and supports. A title alone does not establish impact, recognition, or decision authority. Do not force a person into a single permanent archetype.

## Choose the work

Examine candidate work for organizational importance and the person's distinctive contribution. Challenge comfortable low-impact tasks, visible but unimportant projects, and solutions imported from a previous company without understanding this one.

Look for opportunities to grow others, make a small intervention that unlocks a stuck project, or help valuable work finish. Ask what important outcome would not happen without this person's involvement. Avoid making personal indispensability the objective.

## Operate through others

1. State the outcome and the technical or organizational barrier. Confirm whose priority it is and how success will be recognized.
2. Identify the decision maker, implementers, affected teams, and people with relevant context. Separate influence from authority.
3. Inspect enough technical detail to test the proposal. Write the decisive tradeoff, evidence, alternative, and unresolved disagreement in a short artifact appropriate to the task.
4. Choose where personal implementation is necessary and where coaching, delegation, or alignment has more effect. Keep ownership clear rather than taking over another engineer's project.
5. Help the change reach adoption. Include migration, maintenance, and knowledge transfer when needed; a design document alone is not an outcome.
6. Review results against the original goal and update the approach. Credit contributors and leave a team more capable of proceeding independently.

For career questions, separate skill development, available organizational scope, and promotion recognition. Do not promise that good technical work alone guarantees advancement.

## Deliverable

Return the role hypothesis, selected high-value problem, influence and ownership map, smallest useful intervention, and evidence of impact. Match depth to the user's situation; do not manufacture a company-wide strategy for a local problem.

## Completion check

The plan is ready when role scope is tied to an organizational need, the intervention has a decision owner, and adoption or handoff has observable evidence of completion. For career work, distinguish capability, available scope, and recognition.
