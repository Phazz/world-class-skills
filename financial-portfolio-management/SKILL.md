# Financial portfolio management

Connect investments to the owner's goals, liabilities, and ability to withstand losses. Evaluate the portfolio as a whole before evaluating individual securities. Produce evidence-based proposals; do not impersonate an adviser or imply guaranteed returns.

## Establish the mandate

Ask for the missing information that materially affects the decision:

- Goals, amounts, time horizons, planned contributions and withdrawals, and base currency.
- Cash reserves, income stability, debts, near-term obligations, and other material assets or exposures, including employer equity and property.
- Risk capacity (losses the finances can absorb), risk tolerance (losses the owner can endure), and liquidity needs. Do not substitute one for another.
- Tax residency, account types, withdrawal restrictions, investment exclusions, and any existing investment policy. Never infer residency from language or timezone.
- Holdings, quantities or market values, currencies, account identifiers without credentials, valuation dates, and fees. Request cost basis and tax lots only where relevant.

If these inputs are incomplete, provide a conditional diagnostic and a focused data request. Do not invent target allocations. If a goal appears infeasible, compare saving more, spending less, or extending the horizon before proposing more risk.

## Review process

1. **Reconcile the inventory.** Identify securities precisely, including share class. Convert values using dated FX rates into the base currency. Include cash, reconcile account totals, and avoid counting fund assets twice through look-through analysis. Show gross assets, debt, and net worth separately; flag derivatives and leverage for exposure analysis beyond market-value weights.
2. **Verify evidence.** Record timestamps and sources for prices, FX, fund holdings, fees, and filings. Use official fund documents and primary filings where available. Verify current tax/account rules against the applicable jurisdiction before drawing tax conclusions. Label stale or estimated values; never imply live pricing without live data.
3. **Draft or check the investment policy.** Specify goals, liquidity reserve, eligible assets, target weights summing to 100%, permitted ranges, concentration/leverage limits, benchmark, and review triggers. Keep proposed policy separate from adopted policy. Use scenarios rather than a promised return to assess feasibility.
4. **Inspect combined exposure.** Compare actual weights with policy. Examine issuer, sector, geography, currency, duration, credit quality, and liquidity. Look through overlapping funds where data permits and label incomplete coverage. Include income/employer exposure when relevant. Diversification reduces some risks but cannot eliminate market losses.
5. **Stress the plan.** Consider equity declines, interest-rate changes, inflation, currency moves, income loss, and withdrawals during a downturn. State shock assumptions and calculate effects only with adequate data. Test liquidity, forced-sale risk, leverage, and sequence-of-returns risk. Historical correlations and drawdowns are not worst-case bounds; scenarios are not forecasts or assigned probabilities.
6. **Evaluate results and costs.** Separate contributions and withdrawals from investment gains. Calculate time-weighted or money-weighted returns only when the required valuations and cash-flow dates exist; label the method. Match benchmark period, currency, and dividend treatment. Inspect fund, platform, advice, trading, spread, FX, and tax costs without double counting.
7. **Compare actions.** Include holding steady. For rebalancing, consider contributions or distributions first, then sales where justified by policy drift. Estimate transaction costs and tax consequences, marking unknowns. Show proposed amounts and resulting weights; check liquidity and account restrictions. Keep allocation changes distinct from security selection and market timing.
8. **Record the decision.** Explain what changed, why action is warranted, alternatives, remaining uncertainty, and the next review trigger. Record proposals, approvals, and actual transactions as different states. This skill does not place orders, transfer money, or change accounts.

For recurring or unattended investment reports, read [scheduled reviews](references/scheduled-reviews.md). Use the core review process directly for a one-off analysis.

## Deliverable

Lead with the most consequential finding and decision needed. Include a table of asset class, current value/weight, target/range, drift in percentage points, and proposed change. Follow with concentration/liquidity risks, scenario results, costs and tax uncertainties, evidence dates, and the next review. Avoid precision unsupported by the inputs.

## Completion check

The review is ready when values reconcile to the stated inventory, currencies and dates are explicit, and allocation weights use a consistent denominator. Every proposed change must show resulting weights, costs or cost uncertainty, and policy rationale. Where inputs prevent a calculation or recommendation, mark it unavailable and request the specific missing data.
