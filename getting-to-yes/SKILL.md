# Getting to Yes: negotiate on the merits

Apply a selective synthesis of *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In*. Seek an agreement that serves legitimate interests better than the available alternatives. Reaching agreement is not itself the success criterion.

## Core method

Separate relationship problems from the substance while attending to both. Explore interests beneath stated positions. Generate possibilities for mutual benefit before deciding among them. Use independent, defensible standards instead of turning the discussion into a contest of insistence.

Develop a **BATNA**: the best alternative to a negotiated agreement. It is an action available if this negotiation fails, not an aspiration, threat, or minimum acceptable price. Use its value, costs, risks, and timing to establish a reservation threshold; keep that threshold distinct from the ambitious result you hope to achieve.

## Prepare the decision

1. **Define issues and authority.** Identify what is negotiable, who must approve, the user's constraints, and any real deadline.
2. **Map interests.** For each stated demand, ask what need it serves. Record the user's interests, evidence about the counterpart's interests, and questions for uncertain assumptions. Do not presume all interests are compatible.
3. **Develop alternatives.** List feasible actions if no agreement occurs. Compare them on the same dimensions as the proposed deal. Identify one practical way to improve the strongest alternative. Do not count an unconfirmed offer as secured.
4. **Generate packages.** Vary multiple relevant terms instead of bargaining only over price. Clearly label exploratory packages so they are not mistaken for binding offers. Seek differences in priorities that allow useful trades.
5. **Choose criteria.** Propose relevant benchmarks, precedents, costs, or expert standards. Explain why each applies and examine competing standards fairly. A convenient benchmark is not automatically neutral.
6. **Evaluate the whole agreement.** Compare each package with the BATNA, including implementation burden, uncertainty, and relationship effects. Identify unresolved terms and reject an attractive headline that conceals unacceptable conditions.
7. **Make commitments explicit.** Specify actions, owners, timing, approval conditions, and how changes or disputes will be handled. Recommend agreement, revision, more information, or no deal.

## Handle pressure

Bring positional demands back to interests and criteria. If process tactics obstruct discussion, name the process problem and propose a fair way to proceed. Respect for the person does not require concessions unsupported by the substance.

Do not confuse mutual gains with equal splitting or assume a bargaining range exists. When the other side's limits are uncertain, report a conditional assessment rather than inventing them.

## Deliverable

Return a preparation or evaluation brief: interests, BATNA, target and reservation logic, possible packages, objective criteria, open questions, and recommended next move. Keep private limits out of any counterpart-facing draft unless the user chooses disclosure. Do not send offers or accept commitments without authorization.

## Completion check

The brief is ready when each package is compared with a feasible BATNA on the same dimensions, the reservation logic is explained, and unresolved terms are visible. Where the BATNA or counterpart's limits are unknown, state the condition under which the recommendation holds.
