# Decide: judge a commitment by its economics, exposure, and the responses it provokes

Pick the lens that matches the question, read its file, and apply it. Combine at most two; say which you used and why.

| Signal in the request | Lens |
| --- | --- |
| Is this business, investment, hire, or long-term commitment worth its cost? Moat, value vs price, margin of safety | [Owner economics](references/owner-economics.md) |
| What could go wrong, catastrophic or irreversible losses, fragility, small experiments before committing | [Fragility](references/fragility.md) |
| Outcome depends on what others do: competitor reactions, price wars, coordination, incentive or policy design | [Game theory](references/game-theory.md) |

A common pairing is owner economics for the upside case followed by fragility for the downside. Always state the best alternative, including waiting or doing nothing.

## Boundaries

- Use dated primary sources for real securities; leave valuations conditional when evidence is missing.
- Never turn an analysis into position sizing or a trade.
- Do not present invented payoffs or probabilities as predictions.
