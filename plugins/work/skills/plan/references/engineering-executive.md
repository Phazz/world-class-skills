# The Engineering Executive's Primer: serve the business

Apply a selective synthesis of Will Larson's *The Engineering Executive's Primer* (2024), using public chapter drafts. Evaluate decisions through company outcomes and the relationships required to deliver them.

## Build a shared diagnosis

Hear the CEO, peer functions, and engineering practitioners before accepting one account of the problem. Identify the evidence and incentives behind disagreements. Reconcile compatible observations; make unresolved tradeoffs explicit. A CEO's complaint is important input, but not necessarily a complete causal explanation.

Learn how this company actually makes decisions. Do not transplant a previous employer's staffing, rituals, or support assumptions. Consider whether your own behavior contributes to a conflict.

## Separate planning decisions

Use three connected stages:

1. **Financial plan:** establish the resource envelope with Finance and executive peers. Include headcount, production operations, and development costs. Check hiring assumptions against recruiting and onboarding capacity.
2. **Portfolio allocation:** decide how engineering capacity is distributed across business work and functional needs. Make the tradeoffs visible before debating individual projects.
3. **Roadmap:** agree on achievable scope and sequencing with cross-functional partners inside those constraints.

Keep these discussions distinct enough to make decisions. Reopen an earlier constraint when evidence warrants it rather than making every project conversation a budget negotiation. Planning is ongoing; a changed reality can justify an updated plan.

## Translate into an operating system

- Convert the business goal into engineering commitments, dependencies, and explicit noncommitments. State confidence and what is not yet scoped.
- Assign accountable leaders and decision rights. Specify which decisions stay with teams and which need executive resolution.
- Choose a small set of outcome and health signals. Pair delivery information with reliability, organizational capacity, and cost where relevant; activity counts alone cannot establish business value.
- Establish only the reviews needed to detect drift and resolve decisions. For each recurring meeting or report, name the decision it enables and its owner.
- Draft a concise communication for the appropriate audience: context, tradeoff, recommendation, owner, and decision needed. Drafting does not authorize sending it.

## Failure checks

Does the proposal improve the company or merely protect Engineering's budget? Is a reorganization masking an unresolved disagreement? Does the plan depend on unfilled roles behaving like productive capacity today? Is the executive becoming the decision bottleneck?

## Deliverable

Return an executive brief with business diagnosis, resource constraints, portfolio choices, commitments, leadership responsibilities, and review triggers. Make the requested executive decision clear.

## Completion check

The brief is ready when roadmap commitments fit the stated resource envelope, allocation tradeoffs are explicit, and each unresolved cross-functional decision has a named decision maker or an ownership gap. Keep future hires separate from productive capacity available now.
