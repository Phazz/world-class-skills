# Alex Hormozi: improve the constraint on profitable growth

Apply selected concepts from $100M Offers, $100M Leads, and $100M Money Models. Use the frameworks as testable business hypotheses. The workflow and economic checks below are operational adaptations, not promised results or a reproduction of the books.

## Diagnose and choose a branch

Establish the customer, costly problem, current offer, price, proof of customer results, acquisition channel, and delivery capacity. For an existing business, inspect available funnel, retention, refund, cost, and cash-collection data over a stated period. Mark unknowns; ask only for facts that change the recommendation.

Identify the constraint with evidence:

- **Offer:** buyers encounter the offer but its value, credibility, or terms prevent purchase. Use offer design below.
- **Acquisition:** a credible offer needs more qualified prospects. Use lead generation below.
- **Monetization:** customers buy, but contribution, repeat purchases, or cash timing limit growth. Use offer sequences below.
- **Fulfillment:** demand exceeds the ability to deliver acceptable results. Address capacity or quality before adding demand.

When evidence cannot distinguish the constraints, propose a bounded diagnostic experiment. For a narrow copy or pricing request, produce that artifact using the relevant branch instead of running a complete business review.

## Offer design — $100M Offers

1. **Specify the buyer and outcome.** Name the segment, urgent problem, ability to pay, and means of reaching buyers. Finish with one outcome customers value and evidence supporting that demand, or a validation question.
2. **Apply the value equation.** Improve the desired outcome and perceived likelihood of achieving it; reduce delay and customer effort or sacrifice. Use these as qualitative design levers, not measurable quantities that yield a defensible numerical value score. Tie each proposed improvement to a buyer obstacle.
3. **Build the delivery package.** Map obstacles to useful components, support, or bonuses. Compare customer benefit with fulfillment cost. Finish with a deliverable package, exclusions, dependencies on the customer, and a plausible route to the promised result.
4. **Set terms and test price.** Specify price, payment schedule, and any guarantee with eligibility, remedy, and cost exposure. Use evidence for claims and real limits for scarcity or urgency. Finish with a price hypothesis and an observable test of willingness to pay; attractive copy alone does not validate demand.

## Lead generation — $100M Leads

Use the core four as channel options: warm outreach, public content, cold outreach, and paid advertising. Select a channel based on buyer access, budget, skills, and time to feedback. A lead magnet should solve a narrow relevant problem and make the next paid step understandable.

Define the audience, message, offer or lead magnet, call to action, qualification rule, and follow-up. Track the path from reached prospects to engaged leads, qualified opportunities, customers, and delivered results. State denominators and observation periods so channel comparisons are meaningful.

Apply “more, better, new” as a diagnostic: increase a working activity, improve its conversion, or test a new approach when evidence justifies it. Finish with one bounded channel experiment, its cost and time budget, success measure, and stop condition. Drafting outreach or ads does not authorize sending, publishing, or spending.

## Offer sequences — $100M Money Models

Map what a customer is offered first and what useful offer follows. Consider an entry offer, an upgrade, a smaller alternative, or recurring service only where each solves an identifiable need. Show eligibility, timing, price, delivery burden, and the customer's option to decline or cancel.

Evaluate the sequence through contribution and cash timing. Distinguish booked revenue, collected cash, gross profit, and contribution after acquisition costs. State which costs are included. Estimate customer acquisition cost from attributable acquisition spending and new customers in comparable cohorts; separate observed repeat purchases from speculative lifetime value. Account for refunds, service obligations, and capacity consumed by prepaid offers.

Finish with the proposed sequence and a conservative economic case. If costs or retention are unknown, request the inputs or propose a limited test before recommending scale. Higher order value is useful only when customer outcomes and economics remain acceptable.

## Deliverable and completion check

Lead with the diagnosed constraint and evidence. Return the requested offer, campaign brief, or sequence, followed by assumptions and one next experiment. Include its owner or ownership gap, budget, observation window, success measure, and stopping condition.

The work is ready when the recommendation addresses the identified constraint, claims have evidence or are labeled hypotheses, fulfillment is feasible, and the test can distinguish improvement from failure. Preserve material terms and customer choice; fabricated proof, artificial deadlines, and hidden recurring charges are unacceptable.
