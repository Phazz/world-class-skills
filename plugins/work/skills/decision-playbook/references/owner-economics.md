# Warren Buffett: think like an owner

Apply a synthesis of Buffett's published principles. Do not impersonate him or imply he endorses the conclusion. Adaptations to product, careers, or engineering are analogies, not claims about his advice.

## Decision process

1. **Define the commitment.** Identify what is being purchased or funded, the full cost, the holding period, and the best available alternative. Include waiting as an option.
2. **Establish competence.** Explain how the business creates cash, why customers pay, and what could change that. Separate evidence, estimates, and unknowns. If the economics cannot be explained, recommend further investigation or passing rather than manufacturing confidence.
3. **Examine durability.** Test the proposed competitive advantage against substitution, competition, customer bargaining power, and reinvestment needs. Ask whether growth creates owner value after the capital required to sustain it.
4. **Assess stewardship.** Examine management's incentives, candor, and allocation record. Evaluate retained earnings, acquisitions, distributions, and dilution through their effect on each owner's economic interest, not organizational size.
5. **Distinguish value from price.** Estimate a conservative range from cash that can ultimately reach owners. State the assumptions that drive the range; show a downside case. A wonderful business can still be an unattractive purchase.
6. **Require room for error.** Compare total cost with the conservative value range and alternatives. Explain the specific protection against estimation error or adverse conditions. Avoid invented universal discount thresholds.
7. **Decide and monitor.** Choose commit, investigate, wait, or pass. Specify what would invalidate the thesis. Patience is justified by continuing economics, not by attachment to an earlier decision.

## Agent application

For a nonfinancial commitment, replace price with money, time, attention, and switching costs; replace owner cash with the user's stated durable benefit. State where this analogy breaks down. Do not label an experimental project irrational merely because its outcome is uncertain: evaluate its bounded cost and learning value explicitly.

Use dated primary filings and current facts for a real security analysis. If essential evidence is unavailable, leave a valuation conditional. Never convert this lens alone into position sizing or a trade.

## Deliverable

Produce a short decision memo: recommendation; understandable economics; durability and stewardship evidence; value versus cost; strongest counterargument; next action and thesis-breaking evidence. Make uncertainty visible instead of filling gaps with a precise-looking score.

## Completion check

The memo is ready when the recommendation follows from understandable economics, cost versus conditional value, and the best alternative. Name the strongest counterargument and thesis-breaking evidence. If missing facts prevent valuation, return investigate or a conditional conclusion.
